The City and Me

submitted 14:32 · marking in ink · AI drafts in pencil
Hangzhou is a city that never stops moving. Every morning I watch the busses crawl across the bridge while the river stays calm underneath. My grandmother says the city have changed more in ten years than in the hundred before. When I was young we lived in a small lane house near the market, and the smell of breakfast stalls was my alarm clock. Now the lane is gone. In its place there is a shopping centre with glass walls that reflect the sky but hide the people inside. I do not think the old city was better. I think it was more warmer. The new city gives us speed and choice, but it asks us to pay with something we cannot name until it is gone.
